### Claude Choice

Your `generation system` is your moat. Almost nobody can casually replicate "varied planets, plants, and aliens that feel distinct." That's the thing an indie dev spends years on precisely because it's hard. Whatever you lead with, that should be front and center — it's what makes your game not look like a generic sandbox.
The `physics sandbox` stuff is probably your best second pillar, not a separate feature. Here's why: "explore procedurally generated planets" is a crowded pitch (No Man's Sky owns that mental space). But "build contraptions, vehicles, and chaos on top of a living procedurally generated universe" is a much rarer combo — closer to Scrap Mechanic or Space Engineers crossed with No Man's Sky. That's a hook people don't already have five games for. If your building/physics systems are even half as strong as your generation system, that intersection might be your actual unique selling point.
The `magic system` and the `AI political NPC` system are the riskiest to lean on right now — not because they're bad ideas, but because they're the least developed and the most technically demanding of the five (especially the AI NPC one — believable AI-driven politics/relationships is a genuinely unsolved problem industry-wide). I'd treat those as "depth for players who stick around" rather than what you market on.
The `music system` is a lovely texture feature — it'll make the world feel alive and give players something delightful to stumble into, but it's not going to be why someone buys the game.


### DeepSeek

Hey! Cool ideas. Here's my take:
1. Space game (seed-gen planets, creatures, civs) — This is the most compelling IMO. It directly leverages your existing ManyWorlds engine (world gen, alien gen, building/city gen) and has massive mainstream appeal. The Minecraft x No Man's Sky crossover is still underexplored.
2. Physics sandbox — Extremely fun but also the most technically ambitious. Ropes, bone physics, vehicles, destruction — each is a deep systems rabbit hole. Could be incredible if you have the time.
3. Magic space game — Unique hook with the wards-for-navigation concept. Novel mechanics tend to attract a dedicated audience, but harder to sell broadly.
4. Character/political game — Cool simulation angle but shifts away from your current tech stack. More of a narrative/systems design challenge than an engineering one.
5. Musical game — Niche but passionate audience. Simplified instrument play is a great accessibility win.
My vote: #1, with #2 as a close second if you want to flex the physics side. The synergy with your existing codebase is just too good to pass up on #1.
